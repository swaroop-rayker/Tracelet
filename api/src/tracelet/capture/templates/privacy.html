Privacy notice

You reached this page from a link that records a visit before sending you on to its destination. This notice explains what is recorded, why, and for how long.

Recorded on every visit

These come from the request your browser sends, and are recorded whether or not JavaScript runs.

  • The time of the visit.
  • Your network address. It is never stored in readable form: it is encrypted, and the encrypted copy is deleted after {{ ip_days }} days. A one-way hashed form and the network block it belongs to (not the address itself) are kept, so repeat visits and automated traffic can be recognised.
  • Your network operator, looked up from the network block.
  • The browser, operating system and device type your browser reports.
  • Your language preference, the page that linked here, and any campaign tags in the link.

Recorded only if your browser allows it

  • Screen and window size, pixel density, touch support, processor core count and approximate memory, and the graphics renderer.
  • Your time zone and preferred languages.
  • Short hashes of how your device draws text and graphics. These cannot be reversed into anything about you; they help tell repeat visits and automated traffic apart.
  • Technical markers that tell a person's browser from automated software: whether the browser reports being remotely controlled, how many plugins and fonts it reports, its window width, and traces that automation tools leave on a page.
  • From the details above, three one-way identifiers are derived with secret keys: one to recognise a returning visit, one that changes every day, and one used only to notice the same device appearing from many networks at once. None can be turned back into the details it came from.
  • Your precise location, only if you have already allowed this site to use it in your browser settings. This page never asks for it, and if the permission has not been granted no precise location is recorded.

Never recorded

Names, email addresses, usernames, page content, keystrokes, clipboard contents, contacts, or your browsing history elsewhere. This page sets no cookies and makes no request to any other website.

How approximate location is estimated

Without your permission, location is estimated from your network address using public IP-location databases (MaxMind GeoLite2, IP2Location LITE, IPinfo Lite and DB-IP Lite), the registered name of the network your address belongs to, the name your network operator gives your address, and the location of the network edge that served the request. These estimates are usually regional rather than precise, and where the sources disagree no location is recorded.

{% if external_geo_enabled %}

An external IP-location service (ipwho.is) may also be consulted. It is sent the address of your network — the first address of its block — never your own address, and its answer is reused for everyone on that network.

If you had already allowed this site to use your precise location, those coordinates are sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service to find the street address. Nothing else about your visit is sent.

{% else %}

No external lookup service is consulted: all estimation happens on this server.

{% endif %}

How long it is kept

  • Visit records: {{ visit_days }} days.
  • The encrypted network address: {{ ip_days }} days, after which it is deleted.
  • Aggregate counts, which identify no individual visit, may be kept longer.

Who can see it

Only the administrators of the service that created this link. Reading a network address back in readable form is restricted, limited in how often it can be done, and recorded every time.
